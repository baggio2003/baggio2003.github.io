Theoretical Analysis & System Interpretation

1. Single-Step Increment Models ($X_i$)

Each step $i \in \{1, \dots, n\}$ models an independent interaction between an attacker and the $i$-th target system:

  • Bernoulli Model ($X_i \in \{0,1\}$): Models binary compromise events with success probability $P(X_i = 1) = p$, single-step mean $\mu = p$, and variance $\sigma^2 = p(1-p)$.
  • Rademacher Model ($X_i \in \{-1,+1\}$): Symmetric zero-mean random walk with $P(X_i = +1) = P(X_i = -1) = 1/2$, single-step mean $\mu = 0$, and unit variance $\sigma^2 = 1$.

2. Variance Scaling and the Law of Large Numbers (LLN)

By independence and identical distribution of increments $X_1, \dots, X_n$, the three scaled processes exhibit distinct asymptotic regimes as $n \to \infty$:

  • Total Score ($S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$): $\mathbb{E}[S_n] = n\mu$ and $\text{Var}(S_n) = n\sigma^2$. The variance grows linearly with $n$, causing the trajectory bundle to widen indefinitely ($\text{SD}(S_n) = \sigma\sqrt{n}$).
  • Average Score ($S_n / n$): $\mathbb{E}[S_n/n] = \mu$ and $\text{Var}(S_n/n) = \sigma^2 / n \to 0$. By the Weak Law of Large Numbers, the sample average converges in probability to the true mean: $$\frac{S_n}{n} \xrightarrow{P} \mu \quad \text{as } n \to \infty$$ Visually, the trajectory bundle funnels tightly onto the dashed horizontal line $y = \mu$, and the terminal histogram collapses into a degenerate spike (Dirac delta) at $\mu$.
  • Normalized Score ($S_n / \sqrt{n}$): $\mathbb{E}[S_n/\sqrt{n}] = \sqrt{n}\mu$ and $\text{Var}(S_n/\sqrt{n}) = \sigma^2$. Scaling by $1/\sqrt{n}$ exactly stabilizes the dispersion, keeping the variance constant for all $n$.

3. Central Limit Theorem (CLT) in the Rademacher Case

In the symmetric Rademacher model ($\mu = 0, \sigma^2 = 1$), the normalized sum $S_n / \sqrt{n}$ is already centered with unit variance. By the Lindeberg–Lévy Central Limit Theorem:

$$\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \quad \text{as } n \to \infty$$

When selecting Rademacher and Normalized Score, the red curve on the terminal histogram overlays the exact standard normal density $\phi(z) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-z^2/2}$, demonstrating empirical convergence in distribution.

4. Security Interpretation: $n$ Systems and $m$ Attackers

  • Effect of System Size ($n$): Represents the depth or number of assets in the infrastructure. As $n$ increases, the average compromise rate per system ($S_n/n$) stabilizes around $p$ (LLN), making the expected proportion of breached systems highly predictable for risk assessment. However, the absolute spread of breached systems ($S_n$) scales as $\sqrt{n}$, meaning absolute uncertainty grows with infrastructure size.
  • Effect of Number of Attackers ($m$): Represents the number of independent adversarial campaigns (Monte Carlo sample paths). While $n$ governs the asymptotic law of the stochastic process, $m$ governs empirical resolution: a larger $m$ populates the tails of the distribution, increasing the probability that at least one outlier attacker achieves an extreme penetration score ($\max_{1 \le j \le m} S_n^{(j)}$).
