3. Theoretical Analysis & Cybersecurity Interpretation

3.1 Stochastic Increment Models ($X_i$)

Let $X_1, X_2, \dots, X_n$ be independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables representing the penetration score at each system $i \in \{1, \dots, n\}$:

  • Bernoulli Model ($X_i \in \{0,1\}$): Models binary intrusion outcomes where system $i$ is compromised with probability $P(X_i = 1) = p$ and resists with $P(X_i = 0) = 1-p$. The single-step mean and variance are: $$\mu = \mathbb{E}[X_i] = p, \qquad \sigma^2 = \text{Var}(X_i) = p(1-p)$$
  • Rademacher Model ($X_i \in \{-1,+1\}$): Models a symmetric zero-mean tug-of-war between attacker and defender (e.g., gaining $+1$ privilege level vs. losing $-1$ via detection/remediation) with $P(X_i = +1) = P(X_i = -1) = 1/2$. The single-step moments are: $$\mu = \mathbb{E}[X_i] = (+1)\frac{1}{2} + (-1)\frac{1}{2} = 0, \qquad \sigma^2 = \text{Var}(X_i) = \mathbb{E}[X_i^2] - 0^2 = 1$$

3.2 Comparison of the Three Quantities & Variance Behavior as $n$ Increases

For any linear scaling $Y_n = c_n S_n = c_n \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$, independence yields $\mathbb{E}[Y_n] = c_n n \mu$ and $\text{Var}(Y_n) = c_n^2 n \sigma^2$:

  • Total Score ($S_n = \sum_{i=1}^n X_i$, $c_n = 1$): $$\mathbb{E}[S_n] = n\mu, \qquad \text{Var}(S_n) = n\sigma^2 \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} \infty$$ In the Bernoulli case, $S_n \sim \text{Binomial}(n, p)$ drifts upward linearly with slope $p$; in the Rademacher case, $S_n$ is a symmetric 1D random walk centered at $0$. In both models, the variance grows linearly with $n$ (standard deviation $\propto \sqrt{n}$), causing the trajectory cone to widen without bound.
  • Average Score ($S_n / n$, $c_n = 1/n$): $$\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{S_n}{n}\right] = \mu, \qquad \text{Var}\left(\frac{S_n}{n}\right) = \frac{n\sigma^2}{n^2} = \frac{\sigma^2}{n} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0$$ Dividing by $n$ over-stabilizes the fluctuations: the variance decays hyperbolically at rate $1/n$. By Chebyshev's inequality, for every $\varepsilon \gt 0$: $$P\left(\left|\frac{S_n}{n} - \mu\right| \ge \varepsilon\right) \le \frac{\text{Var}(S_n/n)}{\varepsilon^2} = \frac{\sigma^2}{n\varepsilon^2} \xrightarrow{n\to\infty} 0$$ confirming the Weak Law of Large Numbers (LLN): $S_n/n \xrightarrow{P} \mu$ (convergence in probability to a deterministic constant).
  • Normalized Total Score ($S_n / \sqrt{n}$, $c_n = 1/\sqrt{n}$): $$\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{n}}\right] = \sqrt{n}\,\mu, \qquad \text{Var}\left(\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{n}}\right) = \frac{n\sigma^2}{(\sqrt{n})^2} = \sigma^2 = \text{constant}$$ Scaling by $1/\sqrt{n}$ is the exact critical exponent that keeps the variance constant and non-degenerate ($\sigma^2 \gt 0$) for all $n$. Note that in the Bernoulli case ($\mu = p \gt 0$), the mean $\sqrt{n}\,p$ still drifts to $+\infty$ at rate $\sqrt{n}$, whereas in the zero-mean Rademacher case ($\mu = 0$), both the mean ($0$) and variance ($1$) remain completely invariant with respect to $n$.

3.3 Central Limit Theorem (CLT) in the Rademacher Case

By the Lindeberg–Lévy Central Limit Theorem, for i.i.d. increments with finite mean $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2 \in (0, \infty)$, the standardized sum converges in distribution to a standard normal variable:

$$Z_n = \frac{S_n - n\mu}{\sigma\sqrt{n}} \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1) \quad \text{as } n \to \infty$$

In the Rademacher model, since $\mu = 0$ and $\sigma = 1$, the standardized variable $Z_n$ coincides directly with the uncentered normalized score $S_n / \sqrt{n}$:

$$\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{n}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}\sum_{i=1}^n X_i \xrightarrow{d} \mathcal{N}(0, 1), \qquad \lim_{n\to\infty} P\left(\frac{S_n}{\sqrt{n}} \le z\right) = \Phi(z) = \int_{-\infty}^z \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-u^2/2}\,du$$

As shown in the simulator when selecting Rademacher + Normalized Score, while at early steps ($n/5$, gray histogram) the distribution is visibly discrete and coarse, by step $n$ (blue histogram) the empirical distribution closely matches the red $\mathcal{N}(0,1)$ bell curve, and the Kolmogorov-Smirnov distance $D_n$ decays at the Berry–Esseen rate $\mathcal{O}(1/\sqrt{n})$.

3.4 Context Interpretation: $n$ Systems and $m$ Attackers

  • Role of System Size ($n$ — Infrastructure Scale): $n$ represents the number of defensive layers, hosts, or sequential targets evaluated during an intrusion campaign.
    • Macroscopic Predictability (LLN): In a large enterprise network ($n \gg 1$), the fraction of compromised systems $S_n/n$ converges tightly to the single-system vulnerability rate $p$. Security architects can therefore reliably estimate the average breach ratio $p$ and budget baseline remediation capacity.
    • Absolute Risk Dispersion ($\sqrt{n}$ Scaling): Even though the relative rate $S_n/n$ stabilizes, the absolute number of compromised systems $S_n$ fluctuates around $np$ with standard deviation $\sqrt{n\,p(1-p)}$ (or $\sqrt{n}$ in the Rademacher model). Thus, worst-case absolute damage grows on the order of $\mathcal{O}(\sqrt{n})$.
  • Role of Number of Attackers ($m$ — Adversarial Population): $m$ represents the number of independent threat actors or parallel penetration attempts targeting the infrastructure.
    • Empirical Convergence vs. Tail Exposure: Statistically, $m$ is the Monte Carlo sample size (By the Glivenko–Cantelli theorem, as $m \to \infty$, the empirical histogram converges to the true theoretical distribution at step $n$). Operationally, security is often governed by the most successful attacker $\max_{1 \le j \le m} S_n^{(j)}$: as $m$ increases, the probability that at least one attacker samples the upper tail ($S_n \ge n\mu + k\sigma\sqrt{n}$) approaches $1$. A system defender must therefore provision defenses not just for the mean attacker ($n\mu$), but for the upper quantiles predicted by the Gaussian CLT tail ($n\mu + z_{1-\alpha}\sigma\sqrt{n}$).
